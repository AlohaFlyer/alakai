# ACT Notes Ep 11: It Is a Graph, Not Biology
cast: lehua, derek, pono, kaden
facts: ACT Science is optional, 40 questions in 40 minutes, about 60 s each (act.org; timing.html); Science is scored 1 to 36 and reported separately from the Composite, and a STEM score (average of Math and Science) is reported when Science is taken (act.org Understanding Your Scores); ACT Science content includes biology, chemistry, Earth/space sciences and physics; "Advanced knowledge in these areas is not required, but background knowledge acquired in general, introductory science courses may be needed to correctly answer some of the questions"; reporting categories Interpretation of Data, Scientific Investigation, Evaluation of Models; formats Data Representation, Research Summaries, Conflicting Viewpoints ("two or more explanations for the same scientific phenomena"); "You are not permitted to use a calculator in the science section" (act.org Science Test Description and Preparing-for-the-ACT.pdf, checked 2026-10-16); app strategies "Labels then lookup" (read the question first, read the axis labels and units, find the value or trend, check against the figure, not outside knowledge; trap "Reading the wrong line, column, or unit on a figure"), "Find what changed" (what experimenters changed on purpose, what they measured, what stayed the same; trap "Mixing up the variable that was changed with the one that was measured") and "Sort the views" (each viewpoint's main claim in a few words; trap "Attributing one scientist's claim to another") (data/strategies.json science-data, science-research, science-conflict); skill tips "Find the axis labels and units before the numbers" and "One sentence per scientist" (data/act/skills.json science); the app's Science toggle is in Settings (settings.html); Reading is about 10 minutes a passage (timing.html); app trap label "Trap: misread the question" (assets/engine.js TRAP_NAMES); worked example arithmetic: 2 to 10 cm is 8 cm in 4 weeks (2 cm per week), 6 to 12 cm is 6 cm in 4 weeks (1.5 cm per week); Waialea Bay is called Beach 69 for an old utility pole number (cast.json places)

KADEN: Okay, but what's actually on it? Do I need to know what mitochondria do?
DEREK: Mostly, no. The passages cover biology, chemistry, Earth and space science, and physics, but ACT says you don't need advanced knowledge. A few questions lean on what you learned in an intro science class. Most of it is reading figures. Graphs, tables, and descriptions of experiments. The answer is usually sitting in a figure. And there's no calculator on Science, so the math stays simple. The app's strategy even says it. Check your answer against the figure, not against outside knowledge.
PONO: People lose this section because they panic at the words. Long science words at the top of the passage. You don't need to understand every word. You need to find the number.
LEHUA: So what's the move?
DEREK: Labels, then lookup. Step one, read the question first, so you know which figure it wants. Step two, before you look at any numbers, read the axis labels and the units. What's across the bottom, what's up the side, and what's it measured in. Step three, find the value or the trend the question asks about.
KADEN: Why the units first?
DEREK: Because units can change from one figure to the next. One table in centimeters, one graph in millimeters. The app's trap for this skill is reading the wrong line, column, or unit. Ten seconds on the labels saves you.
LEHUA: Let's do one.
DEREK: Picture a graph. Across the bottom is time in weeks, zero to four. Up the side is plant height in centimeters. Two lines. Plant A starts at two centimeters and ends at ten centimeters after four weeks. Plant B starts at six centimeters and ends at twelve centimeters after four weeks. Question. Which plant grew faster over the four weeks? A, Plant A, because it grew eight centimeters. B, Plant B, because it ended taller. C, Plant B, because it started taller. D, they grew at the same rate.
DEREK: One more time. Weeks across, centimeters up. Plant A, two to ten. Plant B, six to twelve. Both over four weeks. Which grew faster? A, Plant A, because it grew eight centimeters. B, Plant B, because it ended taller. C, Plant B, because it started taller. D, same rate.

KADEN: Eight centimeters. Plant B went from six to twelve. That's six.
DEREK: Same four weeks for both. So A grew eight, B grew six. That's two centimeters a week against one and a half. Plant A grew faster. The answer is A.
DEREK: And B is the trap we're naming today. The app calls it misread the question. B is a true statement about the graph. Plant B did end taller. But the question asked about speed, and B answers a question about height. If you read "faster" and your eyes go to "taller," that's how you lose it.
PONO: Before you look at the choices, put the question in your own words. "Which one changed more." Then you can't get fooled by the tall one.
LEHUA: Okay, next. Experiments.
DEREK: Some passages describe an experiment. The app's strategy is, find what changed. Three roles. What did the scientists change on purpose? What did they measure as the result? And what stayed the same in every trial? In the plant example, if they gave the plants different amounts of water, water is what they changed. Height is what they measured. The kind of soil might be what stayed the same.
KADEN: So the thing they changed goes across the bottom of the graph?
DEREK: Usually, yes. Not always. Check the labels.
LEHUA: And the last kind. Conflicting viewpoints.
DEREK: One passage gives you two or more scientists who disagree. The app's tip is one sentence per scientist. What do they claim, and what fact do they lean on? Jot it on your scratch paper or in the margin. Then most questions are just matching a claim to the right scientist. The trap there is giving one scientist's idea to another.
