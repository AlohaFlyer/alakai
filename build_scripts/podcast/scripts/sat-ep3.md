PONO: Two passes. First pass, you ride through in order. You look at a question. If you can see the path in about ten seconds, you take it. If you can't see the path, you pick your best guess, you flag it, and you move on. You do not sit there. Sitting there is fighting the horse.

LEHUA: And when you guess, cross things out first. The app has a little x next to every choice. Use it.
DEREK: Right. Crossing out one wrong choice turns a one-in-four guess into one-in-three. Cross out two and it's a coin flip. Small moves, real points. One warning: only cross out a choice when you can say why it is wrong. If you cross out the right answer, the app will tell you, and that's a sign you were guessing with the x button, not thinking with it.
LEHUA: Say the guessing rule, Derek, because people still don't believe it.
DEREK: There is no guessing penalty. None. On the SAT and on the ACT. A blank is a guaranteed zero. A random guess on a four-choice question is worth a quarter of a point on average. And if you can cross out even one wrong choice, it's a third. So a blank is the only answer that is always wrong.
KADEN: So I should just guess on everything?
DEREK: You should answer everything. Guessing is what you do when you've run out of better options. But the rule is, no blanks. Ever. Pau. Done.
PONO: Second pass. You got time left, you go back to the flags. You already have an answer on every one of them, so you're not rescuing anything, you're just upgrading. Start with the one that looked closest. Leave the monster for last. If the clock runs out while you're on the monster, who cares, you already answered it.
LEHUA: And on the digital SAT the flag is built in. There's a button. And there's a little grid at the bottom that shows you every question and whether you answered it. Use the grid. That's your dashboard.
KADEN: What about checkpoints? My tutor said something about checkpoints.
PONO: Your tutor's right. You look at the clock exactly twice. Not every thirty seconds. Twice. At the one-third mark and the two-thirds mark. And you ask one question. Am I at or past the target?
DEREK: Reading and Writing, that's question nine at ten minutes forty, and question eighteen at twenty-one minutes twenty. Math, question seven at eleven forty, and question fifteen at twenty-three twenty. Those are in the app on the Time Squeeze page, you don't have to memorize them right now.
PONO: If you're behind, you flag faster. That's it. You don't panic, you don't speed-read, you just lower the bar for what you'll flag. If you're ahead, you slow down one notch. Being ahead means you're probably rushing.
KADEN: Being ahead is bad?
PONO: Being ahead on a horse means the horse is running you.
LEHUA: Let's talk about what happens in the last sixty seconds, because that's where people lose points they already had.
DEREK: In the last minute, you stop solving. Full stop. You open the grid, you look for anything unanswered, and you put something in. Then you stop. A half-finished solution is worth zero. A guess is worth something.

DEREK: If you have it, you have it. Finish it, then check the grid. But "I have it" means you're writing the answer, not "I think I see where this goes."
